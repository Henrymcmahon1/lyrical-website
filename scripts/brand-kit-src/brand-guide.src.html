01 · The mark

Approximately equal, not equal

The mark is the mathematical sign for approximately equal to. That is the whole argument in one character: a version in another language is not the identical record, and claiming it is would be dishonest. Two modulated waves, the lower one diverging slightly from the upper, because they are close but not the same.

It is generated, never drawn. The geometry lives in code and every file in this kit was produced from it, so an exported logo cannot drift from the website.

Do

Clear space: keep a margin of at least half the mark's height on every side. On a social avatar, leave more: both Instagram and LinkedIn crop to a circle.

Do

Minimum size: 24px tall on screen, 8mm in print. Below that the taper stops reading and it looks like a smudge.

Do

Use one flat colour. Both strokes are always the same colour as each other.

Never

Never put anything inside the mark. It has no counter. The gap between the waves is not an enclosed shape, it is the background showing through, so text or an image placed there sits on top of the strokes and disappears. This is the single most common mistake with it.

Never

Never rotate, stretch, outline, add a shadow, or recolour the two strokes differently from each other.

Never

Never redraw or trace it. Use the files in logo/. If you need a size that isn't there, ask, and it gets generated from the geometry.

Never

Never place indigo on the dark ground. It measures 2.7:1, which fails legibility. Use the dark accent there instead.

03 · Typography

Two voices, and they do not swap

Brand voice

Fraunces

Headlines, the wordmark, pull quotes, anything display. Weight 600, never lighter: at 400 the headlines look thin and unfinished. Fraunces was chosen for its optical-size axis, meaning it is drawn differently at 16px and 72px, so let it size naturally rather than compensating with letter-spacing.

Product voice

Archivo

Body copy, interface labels, captions, forms, decks. Regular for running text. Keep lines near 65 characters; longer and the eye loses its place returning to the left edge.

Labels and figures

Monospace, uppercase, letter-spaced

Small eyebrow labels and anything numeric. Always with generous letter-spacing, always small. Use tabular figures wherever numbers stack in a column.

Both faces are licensed under the SIL Open Font License, so they are free to use commercially, embed in documents and self-host. The fonts/ folder has the web files; install the desktop versions from Google Fonts for Word, Keynote or Figma. Never load them from a CDN on the website.

04 · Voice

What we say, and what we never say

Lyrical sells trust, not technology. Its own strategy is explicit that authorisation, not production capability, is the binding constraint in this category. The words have to behave accordingly, because a rights holder's lawyer reads them.

Say

Recreated. Re-sung. Performed. Transcreation. The promise is that it does not sound like a machine, so machine language argues against the product.

Never

"AI-generated". Or synthesised, cloned, or deepfaked. Not a euphemism problem, a positioning problem.

Never

Population or reach multipliers. No "reaches 6.4× more listeners". Population figures are an internal prioritisation heuristic and presenting them as reach is a claim we cannot support.

Never

Another artist's name as an example, and never one artist's voice described on another's catalogue. Everything we show is authorised, so everything we say must be too.

Never

"Solutions" as a label for what we do. It says nothing and it sounds like enterprise software.

Say

Permission first. Authorisation leads, in any deck, page or email. It is the reason to choose us, not a disclaimer at the bottom.

Do

Write plainly, in short sentences. Concrete nouns beat adjectives: "a finished mix and a dry vocal stem" beats "premium deliverables".

Do

Use British spelling, sentence case for headings, and no em-dashes in anything a customer reads.

Do

State the limit when there is one. Saying what we do not do is the cheapest trust we can buy.

Never

Never use urgency or scarcity. A buyer deciding whether to trust us with masters gets more cautious under pressure, not less.

05 · Motion

Motion carries the idea, or it does not run

  • Transform and opacity only. Never animate width, height, margin or position: those force layout and stutter on a phone.
  • One idea per moment. The pause symbol becoming the mark is the signature: a held note resolving into approximately equal. Everything else stays quiet so that reads.
  • Scroll-linked, not autoplay. Motion responds to the reader rather than performing at them.
  • Nothing ever traps the reader. No pinned section on a phone, no scroll-jacking, no modal that cannot be dismissed.
  • All of it off under prefers-reduced-motion, and the page must be fully readable with JavaScript disabled.
  • Never build an aperture or punch-through from the mark. See the mark rules: there is no counter to punch through.
